  • Active Bonus

    Active Bonus

    p25

    Ability cards that stay in your Active Area with ongoing effects.

    • Persistent: Stays until all slots used or scenario ends. Place token on first slot, move each trigger.
    • Round: Active until end of round, then goes to discard/lost.

    Cards in Active Area count as discarded/lost (depending on symbol). You can voluntarily end any active bonus by moving the card to discard/lost.

  • Advantage

    Advantage

    Positivep20

    Draw two Attack Modifier cards, use the better one.

    Which is better:

    • Higher number wins
    • ×2 beats any number, Null loses to any number
    • Positive effects (elements, conditions) are better but undefined
    • If ambiguous: use first card drawn

    With Rolling Modifiers:

    • One rolling + one normal: rolling adds to chosen card
    • Two rolling: keep drawing, all effects apply
    Advantage + Disadvantage = cancel out, draw one card normally.

    Main source: Strengthen condition.

  • Adjacent

    Adjacent

    Hexes sharing a border with no wall between them.

    • Wall between = NOT adjacent
    • Closed door between = NOT adjacent
    • Only sharing corner = NOT adjacent
    • Flying figure on obstacle = NOT adjacent to that obstacle
  • Ally

    Ally

    Any figure on your side (other characters and their summons).

    You are NOT your own ally!

    "Heal all allies" does NOT heal you. "All allies gain Shield" doesn't include you. Use "self" to mean yourself.

  • Area of Effect (AoE)

    Area of Effect

    p21

    Attacks affecting multiple hexes shown by a pattern.

    • Grey hex: Your position → Melee AoE
    • Red hexes: Target hexes, always in range if attack is valid
    • No grey: Ranged AoE, place pattern within range

    Can rotate/mirror pattern. MUST target all enemies in red hexes you have LOS to. Draw separate modifier per target. Allies never hit unless stated.

  • Attack

    Attack X

    p19-22

    Deal damage to enemies. Only "Attack" abilities are attacks—"deal/suffer damage" effects are different.

    Types:

    • Melee: No Range = Range 1 (adjacent only)
    • Ranged X: Up to X hexes with LOS

    Steps:

    1. Declare target(s) in range + LOS
    2. Apply bonuses (+1 if target Poisoned)
    3. Draw modifier
    4. Apply target's Shield
    5. Deal damage + effects
    6. Resolve Retaliate
    Ranged attack on adjacent enemy = Disadvantage

    Can't attack while Disarmed. Null = 0 damage but effects still apply.

  • Attack Modifier Deck

    Attack Modifier Deck

    p11

    Draw a card for each attack to modify damage.

    Standard deck (20): 6× +0, 5× +1, 5× -1, 1× +2, 1× -2, 1× ×2, 1× Null

    Special cards:

    • Null (×0): 0 damage, but effects still apply
    • ×2: Double damage
    • Bless: Like ×2, removed after draw
    • Curse: Like Null, removed after draw
    • Rolling: Keep drawing until non-rolling

    Shuffle at round end after drawing ×2/Null. Improve with Perks.

    Battle Goal

    p40

    Secret personal objectives per scenario.

    • Draw 2 at start, keep 1 secretly
    • Complete during successful scenario → earn checkmarks
    • 3 checkmarks = 1 Perk
  • Bless

    Bless

    Positivep23

    ×2 card shuffled into modifier deck.

    • Not a condition—can't be removed by condition removal
    • Removed from game after drawing
    • Max 10 per deck

    Sources: Sanctuary, abilities, items.

  • Boss

    Boss

    p33

    Unique powerful enemies with special rules.

    • NOT elites—"elite" abilities don't affect them
    • Own ability deck
    • Often immune to certain conditions
    • "C" = number of characters
C
  • Card Selection Phase

    Card Selection Phase

    p16

    At the start of each round, all players simultaneously select which cards to play.

    Options:

    • Two Cards: Choose 2 ability cards from your hand. One will be your leading card (determines initiative).
    • Long Rest: Instead of playing cards, declare you're resting (Initiative 99).

    Communication Rules:

    • ✓ You CAN discuss general strategy ("I'll go early and tank")
    • ✓ You CAN share your intentions ("I'll heal someone this round")
    • ✗ You CANNOT reveal exact initiative numbers
    • ✗ You CANNOT name specific cards you're playing

    Once all players have selected, reveal initiatives and determine turn order.

  • Character Turn

    Character Turn Structure

    p18-19

    On your turn, perform the top action of one card and the bottom action of the other (in either order).

    Turn Structure:

    1. Start of turn: Resolve any "start of turn" effects (like Wound damage)
    2. Actions: Perform your two actions in any order
    3. End of turn: Loot your hex, resolve "end of turn" effects, remove expiring conditions

    Default Actions:

    Instead of using a card's printed action, you can always use:

    • Top: Attack 2 (melee)
    • Bottom: Move 2

    After Your Turn:

    • Non-lost cards go to your discard pile
    • Lost cards (🗙 symbol) go to your lost pile
    • Cards with active bonuses stay in your Active Area
  • Conditions (Overview)

    Conditions

    p22-23

    Status effects on figures. Most removed at end of affected figure's next turn.

    Negative:

    • Poison — +1 damage on attacks against you
    • Wound — 1 damage at start of turn
    • Immobilize — Can't Move
    • Disarm — Can't Attack
    • Stun — Can't use abilities or items
    • Muddle — Disadvantage on attacks
    • Curse — Null card in deck

    Positive:

    • Invisible — Can't be targeted
    • Strengthen — Advantage on attacks
    • Bless — ×2 card in deck
    Poison/Wound only removed by Heal. Applying a condition someone already has just refreshes its duration.
  • Curse

    Curse

    Negativep23

    Null (×0) card shuffled into modifier deck.

    • Removed from game after drawing
    • Max 10 per deck
    • Even with immunity, already-present Curse still causes 0 damage
  • Campaign

    Campaign

    p46-48

    Persistent world where decisions carry over between scenarios.

    Includes:

    • City and road events between scenarios
    • Town prosperity level (1–9)
    • Character progression and retirement
    • Unlocking new scenarios and classes
    • Map stickers tracking world state

    Scenario outcomes affect which future scenarios and options are available.

  • City Events

    City Events

    p46

    Draw and resolve 1 city event card between scenarios (after returning from the road).

    • Two options (A/B) with different consequences
    • Outcomes can affect gold, items, prosperity, or unlock scenarios
  • Coin Tokens

    Coin Tokens

    p27

    Money tokens placed on the map when monsters die.

    • Automatically looted when you end your turn on them
    • Value × gold multiplier for the scenario level = gold earned
    • Tokens left on the map when the scenario ends are lost forever

    See Scenario Level for the gold multiplier table.

  • Damage

    Damage

    p28

    When suffering damage, choose ONE:

    • Take HP damage on dial (0 HP = Exhausted)
    • Lose 1 card from hand
    • Lose 2 cards from discard pile

    Can mix methods. May lose cards BEFORE using defensive items/abilities. Monsters/summons can't lose cards.

  • Difficult Terrain

    Difficult Terrain

    p14

    Costs 2 movement to enter (not leave).

    Jump ignores except final hex. Flying ignores entirely. Push/Pull moves through normally.

  • Disadvantage

    Disadvantage

    Negativep20

    Draw two modifiers, use the worse one.

    • Lower number is worse, Null worst, ×2 best
    • If ambiguous: use first card
    • One Rolling: ignore it entirely
    • Two Rolling: keep drawing, only LAST rolling effect applies

    Causes: Muddle, ranged attack on adjacent enemy.

  • Disarm

    Disarm

    Negativep23

    Cannot perform Attack abilities.

    Immediate effect. Removed at end of turn. Disarmed monster still moves toward enemies.

  • Door

    Door

    p19

    Closed: Functions as wall. Only characters can open by moving onto it.

    When opened:

    1. Reveal new room + monsters
    2. Place monsters per player count
    3. Randomize standee numbers
    4. Draw Monster Ability Cards for new types
    5. Monsters with lower initiative act after you

    Open: Empty hex, move through freely.

E
  • Elements

    Elements

    p23-24

    Six elements: Fire, Ice, Air, Earth, Light, Dark. Tracked on Element Board.

    States: Inert (not available) → Waning (available) → Strong (fully available)

    Infusing (creating):

    • Perform ability with element icon → element goes to Strong
    • Happens at END of your turn
    • Cannot infuse AND consume same turn

    Consuming:

    • Element at Strong or Waning can be consumed for bonus effects
    • Consumed element goes to Inert
    • Optional—can skip the bonus

    End of Round: All elements shift left (Strong→Waning→Inert).

  • End of Round

    End of Round

    p29

    After all figures have taken their turns, perform these steps in order:

    1. Trigger end-of-round effects: Any abilities that say "at end of round" trigger now
    2. Remove round bonuses: Move any cards with round bonuses from Active Areas to discard/lost piles
    3. Short Rest: Characters may optionally take a Short Rest
    4. Shift elements: Move all elements one step toward Inert (Strong→Waning→Inert)
    5. Shuffle modifiers: If any modifier deck drew a ×2 or Null, shuffle that deck now
    6. Collect ability cards: Return all Monster Ability Cards to their decks, shuffle if needed

    After completing these steps, a new round begins with card selection.

  • Exhaustion

    Exhaustion

    p28

    Character is out of the scenario when:

    • HP reaches 0
    • Can't play 2 cards (Two Actions) or Long Rest

    Remove figure from map, no more turns. Still keep earned gold/XP.

    ALL characters exhausted = scenario lost!
  • Experience (XP)

    Experience (XP)

    p27, p42

    Experience points allow your character to level up and gain new abilities.

    Gaining XP during scenarios:

    • Ability cards: Many cards have XP symbols—gain that XP when you perform the ability
    • Scenario completion: Successful scenarios award bonus XP based on scenario level
    • Summons using abilities with XP give that XP to the summoner

    XP and Leveling:

    • Track XP on your character sheet (XP is permanent, never lost)
    • When XP reaches the threshold for the next level, you level up between scenarios
    • Level thresholds: Lvl 2: 45 XP, Lvl 3: 95 XP, Lvl 4: 150 XP, Lvl 5: 210 XP, Lvl 6: 275 XP, Lvl 7: 345 XP, Lvl 8: 420 XP, Lvl 9: 500 XP

    Level Up Benefits:

    • Increase max HP (see character mat)
    • Choose 1 new ability card from your level or below
    • Gain 1 Perk
    XP gained during a scenario counts even if you become Exhausted or the scenario fails.
  • Enhancement

    Enhancement

    p44-45

    Permanently improve an ability card at the Enhancement Shop in campaign mode.

    • Cost depends on the type of enhancement and the card's level
    • A sticker is placed directly on the card
    • The enhancement stays with the character forever — even after retirement
    Enhancements are one of the most powerful long-term investments in the game. Plan them carefully as costs increase for higher-level cards.
F
  • Flying

    Flying

    p19

    Ignores figures, traps, hazards, difficult terrain, obstacles during movement.

    • Must end in unoccupied hex
    • Can end on trap/obstacle if you have permanent Flying
    • Lose Flying while on obstacle? Take damage, move to nearest empty hex
  • Figure

    Figure

    Any playing piece on the map: characters, monsters, summons, and scenario allies.

    Types of figures:

    • Characters: Player-controlled heroes
    • Monsters: Enemies controlled by AI, including bosses
    • Summons: Allies created by abilities, follow monster AI
    • Scenario Allies: NPCs that help in specific scenarios

    All figures can occupy hexes, be targeted, and block movement (except when using Flying or Jump).

  • Gold Conversion

    Gold Conversion

    p27

    Na afloop van een succesvol scenario worden looted coin tokens omgezet naar gold.

    Formule:

    Coin waarde × goud-multiplier = gold

    Multipliers per scenario level:

    LevelMultiplier
    0–1×2
    2–3×3
    4–5×4
    6–7×5

    Voorbeeld: Je lootte 3 coins à waarde 2 op scenario level 3 → 3 × 2 × 3 = 18 gold.

    Tokens die op de grond liggen als het scenario eindigt zijn voorgoed verloren — loot ze altijd voor het doel wordt bereikt!
  • Gold

    Gold

    p27, p43

    Gold is the currency used to buy items and make donations.

    Gaining Gold:

    • Looting money tokens: Each token = (token value) × (gold multiplier for scenario level)
    • Selling items: Sell for half purchase price (rounded down)
    • Scenario rewards: Some scenarios grant bonus gold

    Gold Multipliers by Level:

    Level 0-1: ×2 | Level 2-3: ×3 | Level 4-5: ×4 | Level 6-7: ×5

    Spending Gold:

    • Buy items at the shop (between scenarios)
    • Donate to Sanctuary (10 gold for 2 Bless)
    • Enhance ability cards (permanent upgrades)
    Gold is personal—you cannot trade gold with other players!

    Hazardous Terrain

    p14

    Deals damage when entered: (2 + Level) ÷ 2 rounded down.

    • Tile stays after triggering (unlike traps)
    • Flying ignores it
    • Jump ignores except destination
  • Heal

    Heal X

    p26

    Restore X HP to a figure.

    • Heal Self: Only yourself
    • Heal Range Y: Self OR one ally in range + LOS

    With conditions:

    • Wound only: Remove Wound + restore HP
    • Poison only: Remove Poison, NO HP restored
    • Both: Remove both, NO HP restored
    Long Rest Heal follows same rules!

  • Immobilize

    Immobilize

    Negativep22

    Cannot perform Move abilities.

    Can still be pushed/pulled. Teleport isn't Move so works. Removed at end of turn.

  • Initiative

    Initiative

    p18

    Determines turn order. Lower number goes first.

    • Characters: Number on leading card
    • Monsters: Number on Monster Ability Card
    • Long Rest: Initiative 99

    Same monster type: Elites before normals, then by standee number.

    Ties: Character before monster. Characters compare non-leading cards. Players choose for monsters.

    Summons act immediately before summoner.

  • Invisible

    Invisible

    Positivep23

    Cannot be focused or targeted by enemies. Monsters treat you as obstacle.

    Still works against Invisible:

    • Retaliate (not targeted)
    • Traps and hazards

    Removed at end of turn.

  • Items

    Items

    p8, p28

    Slots: Head, Body, Legs, Two Hands (or 2× One Hand), Small Items (half level, rounded up)

    Can't use if: Stunned, item spent/consumed, during start/end of turn effects.

    Spend (⟲): Rotate card. Refresh with Long Rest.

    Consume (✕): Flip facedown. Only special abilities recover consumed items.

    Jump

    p19

    • Final hex treated as normal movement (Difficult costs 2, traps trigger)
    • Must end in unoccupied hex

  • Level (Character)

    Character Level

    p42-44

    Your character's level determines their power and available abilities.

    Level affects:

    • Maximum HP (see character mat for your class)
    • Which ability cards you can choose from
    • Number of Perks you've earned
    • Number of Small Item slots (level ÷ 2, rounded up)
    • Scenario level calculation for the party

    Leveling Up:

    When your XP reaches the threshold, level up between scenarios:

    1. Increase max HP to new level value
    2. Choose 1 new ability card from your level or below
    3. Gain 1 Perk

    Maximum character level is 9.

  • Line of Sight (LOS)

    Line of Sight

    p19

    Required for attacks and ranged abilities.

    1. Pick any corner of your hex
    2. Pick any corner of target hex
    3. If a line between them doesn't touch walls/closed doors, you have LOS

    Figures and obstacles don't block LOS. Partial hexes count as walls.

  • Long Rest

    Long Rest

    p17

    Alternative to Two Actions. Initiative 99.

    Requirement: ≥2 cards in discard pile.

    On your turn:

    1. Lose 1 card from discard (your choice)
    2. Return rest of discard to hand
    3. Optional: Heal 2 Self
    4. Optional: Refresh spent items
    Can still use items during Long Rest turn! Summons still act before you.
  • Loot

    Loot X

    p27

    Pick up money tokens and treasure tiles within X hexes + LOS.

    Automatic Looting:

    At the END of your turn, you MUST pick up all money tokens and treasure tiles in your hex. This is essentially a free "Loot 0" every turn.

    What Can Be Looted:

    • Money tokens: Gain gold equal to token value × gold multiplier
    • Treasure tiles: Gain the reward listed in the scenario book (gold, items, or special rewards)

    Important Rules:

    • Money drops where a monster dies
    • Only the character who loots a treasure gets its reward
    • Summons cannot loot anything
    • Items gained from treasure can't be used until next scenario
    Money/treasure left on ground when scenario ends is LOST FOREVER! Make sure to loot before completing the objective.
  • Lost Cards

    Lost Cards

    p28

    Cards in your Lost pile cannot be recovered through normal means. They're gone for the rest of the scenario.

    Cards go to Lost when:

    • You play an ability with the Lost symbol (🗙)
    • You choose to lose cards to negate damage
    • You lose a card during Long Rest or Short Rest
    • A persistent Active Bonus uses all its slots

    Special Recovery:

    Some rare abilities can recover lost cards, but these are uncommon. Plan your lost abilities carefully!

    Running out of cards causes Exhaustion! Each lost card brings you closer to being unable to continue.
M
  • Monster Ability Cards

    Monster Ability Cards

    p29

    Each monster type has its own deck of ability cards that determine what monsters of that type do each round.

    Drawing Ability Cards:

    • At the start of each round, draw one card for each monster type currently on the board
    • ALL monsters of that type use the same card (same initiative, same actions)
    • If a new monster type is revealed mid-round (e.g., opening a door), draw a card for them immediately

    Reading the Card:

    • Initiative: Number at top determines turn order
    • Actions: List of abilities performed top to bottom
    • Shuffle symbol: If present, shuffle the deck at end of round

    Special Values:

    • +X / -X: Add to the monster's base stat
    • No number: Use base stat from stat card
    • –: The monster doesn't perform this action
  • Monster Type (Normal vs Elite)

    Normal vs Elite Monsters

    p29

    Most monster types come in two versions: Normal and Elite.

    Differences:

    • Elite monsters have higher HP, attack, and often extra abilities
    • Elite standees have a gold border
    • Use the corresponding row on the monster stat card (Normal or Elite)

    Turn Order:

    Within the same monster type, Elite monsters act before Normal monsters. Then by standee number (lower numbers first).

    Setup:

    Scenario books show which version to use based on player count. White = 2 players, Yellow = 3 players, Red = 4 players.

  • Monster Focus

    Monster Focus

    p29-31

    Monster Focus is one of the most complex rules in Gloomhaven. Understanding it well helps you predict monster behavior and position yourself strategically.

    The Core Question: Which enemy can this monster attack using the least amount of movement?

    Step 1: Identify Valid Targets

    The monster looks at ALL enemies (usually characters) and asks: "If I had unlimited movement, which enemies could I eventually reach and attack?" This creates a list of potential focus targets.

    • Invisible enemies are completely ignored—monsters can't focus on them at all
    • There must be an unblocked path to a hex where the monster could attack from
    • If there's literally no path (completely walled off), that enemy isn't a valid target

    Step 2: Calculate Movement Needed

    For each valid target, the monster calculates: "How many hexes do I need to move to reach a position where I can attack this enemy?"

    • Melee monsters: Need to reach a hex adjacent to the target
    • Ranged monsters: Need to reach a hex within their attack range AND with line of sight
    • If the monster's ability card has no attack, treat it as a melee attack (Range 1) for focus purposes

    Step 3: Choose the Focus

    The monster focuses on the enemy requiring the LEAST movement. This is NOT necessarily the closest enemy by distance—it's about how many hexes the monster needs to actually move.

    Example: A melee monster is 3 hexes from Character A, but there's an obstacle in the way requiring 5 movement to walk around. Character B is 4 hexes away with a clear path. The monster focuses on Character B (4 movement) not Character A (5 movement).

    Breaking Ties:

    If multiple enemies require equal movement:

    1. Proximity: Focus the one with shortest direct distance (count hexes in straight line, ignoring obstacles)
    2. Initiative: If still tied, focus the one with lowest initiative this round
    3. Players Choose: If STILL tied, players decide which enemy becomes the focus

    Path Rules:

    • Monsters consider traps and hazardous terrain as obstacles when calculating paths—they try to avoid them
    • EXCEPTION: If avoiding a trap/hazard makes a target unreachable, the monster WILL path through it
    • Monsters CAN path through other monsters and characters (they just can't end there)
    • Difficult terrain counts as 2 movement when calculating paths
    No Focus? If a monster cannot find ANY valid target (all enemies are Invisible, or completely unreachable), the monster has no focus. It will NOT move and will NOT attack. However, it WILL still perform any other abilities on its card (like Shield, Heal, creating elements, etc.).

    Common Mistakes:

    • ❌ "The monster attacks whoever is closest" — Wrong! It's about movement to attack position, not raw distance
    • ❌ "Ranged monsters focus whoever is in range" — Wrong! They still calculate movement, even if multiple targets are already in range
    • ❌ "Monsters always avoid traps" — Wrong! They will path through traps if it's the only way to reach ANY target

    See also: Monster Movement for what happens after focus is determined.

  • Monster Movement

    Monster Movement

    p30-31

    After determining focus, the monster moves toward the optimal attack position. Monsters are "smart" about their movement—they always try to optimize.

    Where Does the Monster Want to Go?

    The monster calculates the BEST hex to stand on, considering these priorities in order:

    1. Must be able to attack focus: The destination must be within attack range with line of sight
    2. Avoid Disadvantage: Ranged monsters won't end adjacent to their focus (would cause Disadvantage) if they can help it
    3. Maximize targets: If the attack can hit multiple targets, prefer positions that hit more enemies
    4. Minimize movement: Among equally good positions, use the fewest hexes

    What If the Monster Can't Reach?

    • If the monster doesn't have enough movement to reach its ideal destination, it moves as close as possible along the shortest path
    • If the monster is already in a valid attack position and moving wouldn't improve it, the monster doesn't move at all (saves movement for nothing)
    • If an ally is standing on the monster's preferred hex, the monster stops in the last hex before that ally

    Ranged Monster Movement:

    Ranged monsters have an extra consideration: they HATE being adjacent to their focus because it gives them Disadvantage. If a ranged monster starts adjacent to its focus and has movement available, it will move exactly 1 hex away before attacking (if possible).

    Path Rules:

    • Monsters will NOT move through traps or hazardous terrain unless it's the ONLY way to reach their focus
    • If forced through a trap/hazard, they take the damage/effect
    • Monsters CAN move through other figures (ally or enemy) but cannot end on them
    • Difficult terrain costs 2 movement to enter

    Example: A ranged Archer (Range 3) is adjacent to a character. Its ability card says "Move 2, Attack 2". The Archer will use 1 movement to step away from the character (avoiding Disadvantage), then attack from 2 hexes away. It won't use its second movement point because it's already in a good position.

    Monsters use the minimum movement necessary. They never "wander" or waste movement—every hex must bring them closer to an optimal attack position.
  • Move

    Move X

    p19

    Move 0 to X hexes.

    • Can't move while Immobilized
    • Can't move through walls or closed doors
    • Can move through allies, not enemies
    • Can't end on another figure
    • Difficult: costs 2 to enter

    Jump/Flying ignore figures and terrain.

  • Muddle

    Muddle

    Negativep23

    Disadvantage on all attacks.

    Removed at end of turn. Stacks with other Disadvantage sources (but Advantage cancels it).

N
  • Negative Hex (Self)

    Negative Hex / Negative Targeting

    Some abilities can target yourself with negative effects—typically to boost an ally or trigger a beneficial ability.

    Examples:

    • An ability that says "Attack 2, Target all enemies and allies" will hit your allies too
    • Push/Pull can be used on allies if the ability allows targeting allies

    Generally, you cannot target yourself unless an ability specifically says "self" or allows targeting allies (and you're your own ally—wait, you're NOT your own ally!).

  • Null Card

    Null Card (×0)

    The Null card in your Attack Modifier deck causes your attack to deal 0 damage.

    Important:

    • Even with 0 damage, other attack effects STILL APPLY (conditions, push, pull, etc.)
    • Drawing Null triggers a shuffle reminder—shuffle at end of round
    • Curse cards function like Null but are removed after drawing

    The base modifier deck has exactly one Null card. This can't be removed, but Perks that remove -2 cards effectively improve your odds.

O
  • Obstacle

    Obstacle

    p14

    Obstacles are terrain features that block movement. Common examples: rocks, pillars, statues, tables, bookcases.

    Movement Rules:

    • Figures cannot move through or end movement on obstacles
    • Flying figures CAN move through and end on obstacles
    • Jump can move through but NOT end on obstacles
    • Figures cannot be pushed into or through obstacles

    Other Rules:

    • Obstacles do NOT block line of sight
    • A Flying figure on an obstacle is NOT adjacent to that obstacle
    • If a Flying figure loses Flying while on an obstacle, they take trap damage and move to the nearest empty hex
  • Overlay Tiles

    Overlay Tiles

    p14

    Overlay tiles modify hexes with special terrain or objects.

    Types:

    • Obstacles: Block movement
    • Traps: Trigger effects when entered
    • Hazardous Terrain: Damage when entered
    • Difficult Terrain: Costs extra movement
    • Treasure: Can be looted
    • Doors: Connect rooms

    Overlay tiles are placed during scenario setup and may be added/removed during play.

    p44

    Permanent improvement to modifier deck.

    Gain perks from:

    • 3 checkmarks (Battle Goals)
    • Each level up
    • Each previously retired character (+1 for new characters)

    Each perk removes bad cards or adds powerful new ones.

  • Pierce

    Pierce Y

    p22

    Ignores Y points of Shield.

    Example: Attack 4, Pierce 2 vs Shield 3 → Shield reduced to 1 → Deal 3 damage.

    Pierce has no effect if target has no Shield.

  • Poison

    Poison

    Negativep22

    All attacks against this figure gain +1 damage (applied before Shield).

    NOT removed at end of turn. Only removed by Heal (but Heal restores NO HP if Poisoned).

  • Personal Quest

    Personal Quest

    p48

    A secret long-term goal unique to each character.

    • Draw 2 at character creation, keep 1 secretly
    • Completing it causes your character to retire
    • Retiring unlocks a new class and grants prosperity checkmarks and perks
  • Prosperity

    Prosperity

    p48

    The town's overall wealth and development level, ranging from 1 to 9.

    Gaining Prosperity checkmarks from:

    • Character retirements
    • Certain scenario rewards
    • Donations to the Sanctuary

    Each new level unlocks:

    • New items available at the shop
    • New characters can start at a higher level
  • Push / Pull

    Push X / Pull X

    p22

    Move target X hexes away from you (Push) or toward you (Pull).

    • If you apply it, MUST use full distance unless blocked
    • Can't push through/into figures, obstacles, walls
    • CAN push through/into Difficult, traps, hazards
    • Non-flying targets trigger traps/hazards when pushed in

    Push/Pull is optional—can choose not to apply it at all.

  • Range

    Range

    p19

    Count hexes between you and target, including target's hex.

    Walls force you to count around (increases range). Most ranged abilities also require LOS.

  • Retaliate

    Retaliate X

    p26

    Deal X damage back to attacker if they're in range (default Range 1, or specified Range Y).

    Triggers: After attack fully resolves, if retaliating figure survived, and attacker is in range.

    Does NOT trigger if:

    • Retaliating figure killed by attack
    • Attacker pushed out of range

    DOES trigger if: Attacker pulled INTO range.

    Retaliate is NOT an attack: no modifier draw, Shield doesn't reduce it, Poison doesn't boost it.

    Works vs Invisible. Stunned figures with active Retaliate still retaliate.

  • Retirement

    Character Retirement

    p48

    When you complete your Personal Quest, your character retires from adventuring.

    When Retirement Happens:

    • Check your Personal Quest after each successful scenario
    • If completed, your character retires at the end of that scenario
    • You can finish the current scenario normally

    Retirement Benefits:

    • Unlock a new character class (specified on Personal Quest)
    • Add a new City/Road Event card to the decks
    • Gain 1 extra Perk for ALL future characters you create
    • Gain Prosperity checkmarks for the town

    After Retirement:

    • Create a new character (can be any unlocked class)
    • New character starts at Prosperity level (not level 1)
    • New character gets starting gold based on Prosperity
    • Your retired character's items go back to the shop
  • Road Events

    Road Events

    p46

    Draw and resolve 1 road event card when traveling to a new scenario (not when replaying a scenario).

    • Two options (A/B) with different consequences
    • Can affect the party before the scenario starts (buffs, debuffs, gold, etc.)
  • Rolling Modifier

    Rolling Modifier

    p11

    Modifier cards with chain-link symbol. When drawn, keep drawing until non-rolling—add all effects together.

    See Advantage/Disadvantage for special rules.

  • Round Structure

    Round Structure

    p16-18

    Each round of Gloomhaven follows this structure:

    1. Card Selection: All players simultaneously select 2 cards (or declare Long Rest)
    2. Reveal Initiatives: Players reveal their leading cards, draw Monster Ability Cards
    3. Determine Turn Order: Sort all figures by initiative (lowest first)
    4. Take Turns: Each figure takes their turn in initiative order
    5. End of Round: Clean up, shift elements, optional Short Rest

    Repeat rounds until scenario ends (success or failure).

  • Scenario Setup

    Scenario Setup

    p32

    Steps to complete before starting a scenario:

    1. Read the intro text in the scenario book
    2. Set up map tiles and overlay tiles
    3. Place monsters per player count (White=2, Yellow=3, Red=4)
    4. Set monster stats to the current scenario level
    5. Draw monster ability cards for each monster type
    6. Set initiative and HP dials
    7. Draw battle goals (2 per player, keep 1)
  • Scenario Failure

    Scenario Failure

    p38

    Een scenario mislukt wanneer:

    • Alle characters zijn Exhausted voordat het doel bereikt is
    • Een scenario-specifieke faalconditie optreedt (bijv. een NPC sterft)

    Gevolgen:

    • Geen scenario-beloning, geen bonus XP
    • Wel behoud je alle XP en gold die je tijdens het scenario verdiende
    • Je behoudt items en perks
    • Je mag het scenario opnieuw proberen (zonder road event)
    Er is geen permanente "campaign loss" — je kunt elk scenario zo vaak herspelen als je wilt. Alleen de tijd (en je geduld) gaat verloren.
  • Campaign Einde

    Campaign Einde: Winnen & Verliezen

    p48

    Winnen: De Gloomhaven-campagne eindigt officieel wanneer scenario 115 (Gloomhaven) succesvol wordt voltooid. Dit ontsluit de epiloog en de ware eindtekst.

    Verliezen bestaat niet op campagneniveau. Gloomhaven heeft geen harde "campaign over"-staat. Je kunt:

    • Mislukte scenarios herspelen zonder straf
    • Altijd een nieuwe character aanmaken als je huidige retired of exhausted raakt
    • Doorgaan zolang er scenarios beschikbaar zijn

    Scenario-specifieke faalcondities zorgen hooguit voor:
    → Verlies van beloningen van dat scenario
    → Bepaalde story-keuzes die niet meer beschikbaar zijn

    Sommige scenario-uitkomsten sluiten alternatieve paden af. Lees de conclusietekst goed — sommige keuzes zijn permanent!
  • Scenario Completion

    Scenario Completion

    p38-39

    After successfully completing a scenario:

    1. Read the conclusion text in the scenario book
    2. Each character gains the scenario XP bonus
    3. Convert looted coins to gold (value × gold multiplier)
    4. Complete battle goals → earn checkmarks toward Perks
    5. Apply any scenario rewards listed
    6. Mark scenario as complete on the campaign map

    Then proceed to Town Activities before the next scenario.

  • Self (Targeting)

    Self / Yourself

    When an ability says "Self" or "yourself," it can ONLY target you—the figure performing the ability.

    Examples:

    • "Heal 3, Self" — Only heals you, cannot target allies
    • "Shield 2, Self" — Only you get Shield
    • "Move 3, Self" — Only you move (not summons)

    Important Distinction:

    Remember: You are NOT your own ally! So:

    • "Heal 2, all allies" = heals allies, NOT you
    • "Heal 2, Range 3" = can heal you OR an ally
    • "Heal 2, Self" = only heals you
  • Shield

    Shield X

    p25

    Reduces attack damage by X (after Pierce).

    Duration by source:

    • Monster stat card: Always active
    • Monster ability: Until end of round
    • Modifier card: Until end of round
    • Item charge: Just for that attack

    Multiple Shields? Defender chooses order. Stunned figures still benefit from active Shield.

  • Short Rest

    Short Rest

    p17

    Optional at end of round. Requires ≥2 cards in discard.

    1. Random discard card → lost pile
    2. Don't like it? Take 1 HP damage, pick different random card (once per rest)
    3. Return rest to hand

    No healing, no item refresh. Can do in final round.

  • Strengthen

    Strengthen

    Positivep23

    Advantage on all attacks.

    Removed at end of turn. Powerful offensive buff.

  • Stun

    Stun

    Negativep23

    Cannot perform abilities or use items.

    Character: Must Long Rest or discard both chosen cards.

    Monster: Entire turn skipped.

    Active Shield and Retaliate still work while Stunned!

    Removed at end of turn.

  • Summon

    Summon

    p26

    Create ally figure in empty adjacent hex. No empty hex = summon fails.

    Behavior:

    • Acts on your initiative, immediately before you
    • Follows monster AI (not directly controlled)
    • Assumed ability: Move +0, Attack +0
    • Uses YOUR modifier deck
    • Kill credit goes to you

    Removed when: HP=0, summoner exhausted, summoning card recovered/lost.

    Summons can't loot.

  • Target Z

    Target Z

    p21

    Can target up to Z different figures in range + LOS.

    Draw separate modifier for each target. Bonuses apply to all targets.

    On AoE: adds Z-1 targets outside the pattern.

  • Town Activities

    Town Activities

    p43-46

    Between scenarios in Campaign Mode, the party visits Gloomhaven. Steps in order:

    1. Draw and resolve a road event (when traveling to a new scenario)
    2. Visit the city: shop for items, use the Enhancement Shop, donate to the Sanctuary, sell items
    3. Draw and resolve a city event
    4. Level up characters if eligible (sufficient XP)
    5. Create new characters or retire characters whose Personal Quest is complete
  • Trap

    Trap

    p14

    Triggers when entered, then removed from map.

    Damage traps: 2 + Scenario Level

    • Flying doesn't trigger traps
    • Jump ignores except destination
    • Pushing non-flying figures into traps triggers them

    Player-created traps use ability card damage, not level formula.

W
  • Wall

    Wall

    Impassable barriers. Thick black lines between hexes or tile edges. Partial hexes are walls.

    Blocks: All movement (even Flying/Jump), LOS, adjacency, direct range counting.

  • Wound

    Wound

    Negativep22

    Suffer 1 damage at start of each turn. Can kill you if at 1 HP.

    NOT removed at end of turn. Only Heal removes it (HP still restored, unlike Poison).

    Wound + Poison + Heal: Remove both, NO HP restored.
